# 云烟集 · YunYanJi

## Product and non-negotiable constraints

- The user has pivoted from classical-poetry scenes to lonely places: campfire, wind and rain. The active first scene is `rainfire`, a sheltered view of a fire in a rainy forest. Keep the landscape central and the interface quiet. There is no combat, score, account requirement, or gameplay obligation.
- ALL scenic visuals and sounds must be generated by code. Do not add or fetch fixed image textures, HDRIs, skyboxes, sprites, prerecorded audio, video, imported models, or embedded/base64 equivalents.
- Geometry, analytical shaders, seeded noise, runtime-generated CanvasTexture/DataTexture/render targets, procedural environment maps and generated Web Audio PCM are allowed. GPU textures are runtime data, not authored assets. Generate/cache them once where possible; do not regenerate them per frame.
- Use system fonts and native DOM/CSS for UI. No third-party fonts or remote runtime asset dependencies.
- The previous wind/flowers/snow/moon poetry catalogue is no longer the active product direction. The user has explicitly requested snow as one of the active forest's weather states. Keep the old standalone snow world archived; the active forest supports clear, overcast, drizzle, rain, downpour and snowfall without poetry overlays.
- The first rain scene should feel like sitting alone near a warm fire while the larger forest remains cold and wet. Use credible scale, a shared shelter/rain footprint, restrained firelight, wet surfaces, distant trees and layered sound. Avoid miniature cabins, cartoon flame shapes, clutter and invented gameplay goals.
- State clearly whether a scene is a technical foundation or has passed human visual/audio acceptance. Never claim photorealism based on a build passing.

## Architecture

- Vanilla TypeScript, Vite and Three.js. Simulation state must not depend on Three.js objects.
- `src/core/`: seeded randomness, elapsed time and weather state.
- `src/scenes/`: landscape metadata, scene contracts and individually disposable scene implementations.
- `src/render/`: renderer, camera, quality and WebGL lifecycle.
- `src/audio/`: deterministic synthesis and browser audio lifecycle; sound begins only after explicit user activation.
- `src/ui/`: accessible DOM shell and controls; keep implementation terminology out of the default visitor UI.
- Share weather state between visual motion and audio. Keep all time progression independent of frame count.
- `WeatherSimulation` owns weather transitions, seeded lightning and `CampfireSimulation`. Their snapshots drive the same precipitation, sky, mist, fire and sound. Preserve slow wetness/snow changes during live playback; a paused preset offers an immediate preview. Snow is a thin procedural surface coating, not geometric snow depth. The local environment remains a fixed reference probe; restore its reference lights, background, wind, snow and added wood before recapture, then restore the live state.
- Keep the weather panel nonmodal so visitors can watch changes. Fire states are out, embers, small, steady and roaring; four prebuilt added-log slots bound geometry. Cold fuel does not ignite itself; feeding embers rekindles them. The visitor can explicitly choose a fire state. No fuel meters, inventory or survival obligation.
- Thunder arrives on simulation time after its propagation delay. A manual strike must not replace a pending strike before that sound arrives. Reduced motion suppresses flashes. Audio begins only after activation; nine generated loop sources and at most two thunder plus two wood transients are allowed. Mute, pause, reset, hidden tabs and disposal must not leak nodes or replay a backlog.
- Seed every generated scene. Use stable namespace seeds so adding audio randomness does not change the landscape.
- Active foliage comes from `forestGrowth.ts` and `livingForest.ts`; `forestNeedles.ts` remains an archived snow experiment. Runtime-generated bark/soil DataTextures are cached by seed and released with their material references. Preserve their UVs and custom attributes when batching; do not replace generators with authored image data.
- Keep a consistent linear HDR and output-conversion path across quality levels: separate opaque depth, optional half-resolution GTAO/denoise, PBR beauty, lit forest mist, optional HDR bloom, then AgX and sRGB in OutputPass. Apply AO to indirect diffuse/specular light, including a physical material's clearcoat; combine contact and screen estimates without double darkening. Low disables GTAO, bloom and MSAA but retains opaque depth for fire and mist integration. Main-camera depth/AO must not leak into reflection or environment captures.
- `ForestAtmospherePass` explicitly samples `SurfaceOcclusion.depthField.texture`; do not derive its scene depth from whichever colour buffer the composer currently exposes. Integrate scattering and transmittance only to the opaque hit. Medium/high use half-width/half-height targets with 18/28 samples and native sun/fire shadow queries; low uses 35% of each dimension with 10 samples and omits shadow queries. Keep reconstruction depth-aware and time driven by the shared simulation.
- A restrained HDR bloom may support fire and ember radiance; do not use effects or darkness to conceal geometry problems. Ground marked `yyjContactGround` receives the cached contact field but never casts into that field, independently of its ordinary light-shadow flags.
- `ForestEnvironment` first captures the generated sky, then the illuminated local forest, terrain and shelter. Exclude dynamic flames, particles, firelights and emissive radiance from this static probe. Bake ground contacts before capturing the local environment; reproduce that order on context restoration. Capture with wind at rest time/strength zero and a dedicated 1024² sun shadow independent of live quality; temporarily disable the canopy material multiplier, then restore live state in `finally` and invalidate live light shadows. This is one static local IBL approximation, not dynamic global illumination.
- `CanopyLightField` is a cached, blurred estimate of overhead coverage from undeformed forest geometry, shared by indirect surface lighting and mist. Keep its surface contribution restrained because the local environment probe already contains average forest occlusion; never bake that extra multiplier into the probe. It does not attenuate direct sun/fire light and is not a three-dimensional visibility solution.
- `forestWind.ts` shares rooted trunk/leaf deformation between visible, opaque-depth, custom depth and custom distance materials; keep their silhouettes consistent and transform visible normals too. `fireIllumination.ts` supplies one deterministic combustion sample to the flame, coals, lit smoke and both actual point lights. Do not add independent frame-driven flicker to these consumers. Mist uses the two lights' combined energy with the primary light's shadow, an explicit approximation.
- High quality caps drawing at 8,294,400 pixels and DPR 2; medium/low retain 1,500,000/850,000 pixels and their smaller DPR limits. A device-scale screenshot does not bypass the renderer's pixel budget.
- Terrain and fuel remain static; foliage and fire-light positions move. Cache light shadows independently: directional shadows update at up to 6 Hz and both fire point-light shadows at up to 10 Hz in simulation time. Initial rendering, time rollback, quality changes and context restoration invalidate the relevant targets. A zero-delta redraw at time T captures T once if its shadows are stale, then reuses them. Puddle reflections retain an 8 Hz limit for an unchanged view and similarly capture the exact paused time once; camera/projection, rain, target and scene-cache changes may require an earlier capture.
- Dispose GPU/audio resources, event listeners and observers. Handle pause, tab visibility, resize and WebGL context loss.
- Prefer a small working module over speculative frameworks. A new place should reuse the engine and provide a scene module.

## Verification

- Run `npm run check` and `npm run test:browser` before handing off changes.
- Browser checks must include desktop and mobile screenshots, meaningful interaction, asset/network checks and lifecycle behavior. Inspect actual renders, not only DOM text.
- Use `npm run capture` locally for visual iterations; do not push commits merely to obtain CI screenshots. The default is CSS-scale output; add `--dpr 2 --scale device` to inspect full device pixels and compare the report's actual drawing dimensions. Finish a coherent batch locally, then push it for the final quality gate.
- Draft PRs skip CI browser checks; ready PRs retain them. A local screenshot command is not a replacement for the complete browser tests before handoff.
- Performance claims must name hardware/browser and whether the renderer is software-backed. A software-rendered CI run cannot establish real device FPS.
- Keep screenshots/test traces out of shipped assets. Do not commit `dist` or generated media into the source tree.
- Do not introduce a license or change the repository's license without the owner's decision.
